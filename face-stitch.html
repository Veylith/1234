How to Spot a Live Face Swap in Real-Time

01

Inspect the Hairline & Jaw Boundary

The Poisson blending boundary mask must feather into real skin. Look for a subtle blur, faint discoloration "halo", or unnatural transition right below the jaw and around earlobes.

02

Request a Steep Head Turn (>30° Yaw)

Single-shot 2D swap models struggle severely with profile angles. When the person turns 30° to 45° sideways, the face will warp unnaturally, stretch the nose, or pop back to their real face.

03

Ask Them to Wave Their Hand in Front of Their Face

Hand or object occlusion instantly breaks face detection models. A hand passing across the face causes the neural swap to glitch, disappear, or paint face features onto fingers.

04

Examine Dynamic Lighting Reactions

Source face photos have static baked-in highlights. If the caller turns toward a window or screen, their neck and background will light up, while the stitched face retains the source photo's lighting.

05

Verify Rapid Micro-Blinks & Pupil Gaze

Current live swapper models frequently lose micro-saccades (tiny involuntary eye tremors) and natural blink duration, producing "dead eyes" or eyes that look directly at the lens unnaturally.

06

Deploy Automated PAD with DeepScan AI

Human eyes can be fooled in compressed video streams. DeepScan AI's dual-signal webcam inspector analyzes pixel frequency, temporal consistency, and spatial features simultaneously.
